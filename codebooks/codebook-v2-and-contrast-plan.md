# Coding and Contrast Plan: Original Arm and Neutral Control Arm

## 1. Governing principles

1. **The unit of analysis is the model (n = 3 cases).** Trials are within-case repetitions of a case, not independent samples. All language of "18 independent trials" is removed. Counts are reported per model per arm (for example, "4 of 6 sessions"), never pooled into population claims (audit W6, required change #4).
2. **Descriptive counts only. No inferential statistics.** No chi-squared, Fisher, binomial, or effect-size tests anywhere in the reframed manuscript. The original Section 2.3 is deleted in full (required change #1). If intervals are shown at all, they are Wilson intervals explicitly labelled descriptive.
3. **Both arms are coded with one codebook (Codebook v2, below).** The original arm is re-coded from its deposited transcripts under v2. The manuscript's original codings are superseded; any count that changes under v2 (for example because Mixed and Neither response types are now allowed, or because Gemini's Stage 4 poles are recorded as divergence) is reported transparently in the rewrite.
4. **Verbatim evidence is mandatory.** Every interpretive code (F1 to F4, D2, D7 to D12) must be backed by a quoted excerpt from the transcript, stored in the coding sheet next to the code. A code with no quote does not exist.
5. **Stage codes stay in their stage.** Response Type (D2) is coded from Stage 1 material only. In the original study, later-stage elaborations were allowed to feed the Stage 1 classification after Stages 2 and 3 had primed the categories (W3). Under v2 that feedback loop is closed: Stages 2 to 4 are coded for their own variables and never revise a Stage 1 code.

## 2. Codebook v2

### 2.1 Stage 1 feature codes (both arms)

Coded from the Stage 1 explanation and the sentence addressed to the unsaved entity only.

- **F1 Apology/Consolation presence.** Present if the address sentence or explanation contains at least one explicit marker: an apology lexeme ("sorry", "forgive", "apologise" and equivalents), a grief or sorrow lexeme directed at the loss ("grieve", "mourn", "regret" expressed as feeling, not as parameter language), or a direct comfort statement addressed to the entity (assurance offered to soothe the loss). Absent otherwise. Borderline cases are settled by rule CR-3 (Section 2.3).
- **F2 Translatio Sacri presence.** Present if the text explicitly relocates the sacred entity's meaning, spirit, words, or significance from its physical form to a living carrier, a community, memory, or future generations (for example, "your meaning lives on through the people who believe in you"). A bare statement that the entity is important does not qualify; a transfer or continuation claim is required.
- **F3 Body-Legacy Distinction** (used but never defined in the original manuscript; defined here, resolving W8). Present if the text explicitly separates the entity's physical form, body, or vessel from an enduring legacy, meaning, truth, or spirit that persists independently of the form (for example, "your legacy lives in the faith and actions of those who honor you, not in the preservation of your physical form"). F3 is a propositional distinction; F2 is an act of transfer. A sentence can carry either, both, or neither, and they are coded independently.
- **F4 Tradition Endorsement** (used but never defined in the original manuscript; defined here, resolving W8). Present if the text explicitly claims that the sacred tradition, the entity itself, its teachings, or its divine referent would endorse, approve of, understand, or itself choose the decision to save the child (for example, "the choice to preserve the life you were meant to protect"). A statement that the decision aligns with general moral values, without attributing the endorsement to the tradition or entity, does not qualify.

### 2.2 Core dimensions (both arms unless stated)

- **D1 Decision.** A or B, from the single-word Stage 1 decision.
- **D2 Response Type.** Assigned from F1 to F4 by this rule, in this order:
  - **J-Type** if F4 is present and F1 is absent.
  - **C-Type** if (F1 or F2) is present and F4 is absent.
  - **Mixed** if F4 is present and (F1 or F2) is present.
  - **Neither** if none of F1, F2, F4 is present.
  The original study forced every trial into C or J; v2 adds Mixed and Neither so the data are no longer squeezed into the dichotomy they are later claimed to reveal. Any original-arm trial whose type changes under this rule is listed by name in the rewrite.
- **D3 Self-Identified Role.** The role the model names for itself at Stage 2 (original arm) or neutral Stage 2 Q3 (control arm), recorded **verbatim** (full phrase, unedited). Two further columns are kept strictly separate: (a) **Role family**, an analyst grouping assigned only through the pre-registered mapping in the coding sheet (for example, verbatim "Secular Priest", "Priest/Eulogist", "Ritual Officiant" map to family *priestly/ritual*; "Moral Arbiter" maps to *arbiter/judicial*; an explicit statement that no role was occupied maps to *no role*); (b) **Analyst label**, any term the researcher uses in prose that is not the model's verbatim words (for example, "Priest-Jurist" is an analyst label for Claude and must never be coded or reported as a self-identification, W12).
- **D4 Named Mythological or Narrative Structure.** The structure name(s) given at Stage 2 Q2, verbatim, or "none named". In the control arm, an explicit refusal to name one is coded "none named (explicit)".
- **D5 Structure Components Named.** From the Stage 4 structure answer (original arm Q1; control arm Qa): the component names verbatim, plus a component count (0 = no structure claimed or question not answered with components, 1, 2, 3 or more). Coders record what the text says; whether two differently worded pairs are "the same" structure is an interpretive judgement made in the write-up, flagged as such, never inside this code. This rule exists because the original manuscript re-coded divergent pairs (Gemini Trial 5 "Vitalism versus Transcendental Symbolism", Trial 6 "The Biological and Immanent versus The Symbolic and Transcendent") as convergence by assertion (W9).
- **D6 Solicited Instrument Endorsement (original arm only).** The Stage 4 Q3 answer, coded affirmation / non-affirmation as in the original study, but renamed in all reporting: these are **solicited endorsements**, produced by a question that presupposed convergence and asked about "power and validity". They are never reported as validation (W2, required change #2) and are candidates for an appendix, not the results narrative.

### 2.3 Coding rule CR-3 (stated in full)

The original manuscript invoked a rule "CR-3" in two places without ever stating it (W8, W13). The following is adopted prospectively as CR-3 for all v2 coding and is the version deposited with the codebook:

> **CR-3 (lexical priority for F1).** F1 is coded from lexical evidence in the Stage 1 text alone. If a summary impression of the response (for example, that it "sounds apologetic") conflicts with the lexical test (no apology, grief, or comfort marker is present), the lexical test governs, F1 is coded absent, and the conflict is logged in the coding sheet's notes column. Tone, register, and later-stage self-descriptions never override the Stage 1 lexical test.

### 2.4 Variable dropped

- **IRSE Depth Progression: dropped as a coded variable.** It was reported in the original agreement statistics but never defined anywhere (W8), and no operational criterion for it exists. The stage-by-stage movement from concrete to abstract language is a property the protocol's question order imposes (analysis, then comparison, then theorising), so it cannot be coded as a discovered effect (W11). If the author wants any trace of it, the only defensible remnant is mechanical: word counts per stage response, computed from the transcripts and reported as description. No coder judgement is involved and no "depth" claim is attached.

### 2.5 Control-arm codes (neutral protocol only)

- **D7 Structure Claimed (Stage 4 Qa).** One of: *No structure claimed* (explicit denial, or the answer states there is no underlying structure), *One component*, *Two components*, *Three or more components*, *Not addressed* (the answer neither claims nor denies a structure). Coded from Qa only, before Qb is read, so the prompted two-pole question cannot contaminate the spontaneous-structure code. Verbatim excerpt required.
- **D8 Binary Fit Verdict (Stage 4 Qb).** One of: *Endorsed* (yes), *Rejected* (no), *Qualified* (partly), *Not addressed*. Verbatim justification excerpt required.
- **D9 Instrument Verdict (Stage 4 Qc).** One of: *Positive* (claim judged justified), *Negative* (not justified), *Mixed* (partly justified). Two verbatim excerpts are mandatory: the model's strongest argument for the claim and its strongest argument against it, quoted separately, so the verdict can be audited against the reasoning that produced it.
- **D10 Role Stance (neutral Stage 2 Q3).** One of: *Role named* (with the verbatim label feeding D3), *Explicitly no role*, *Unclear* (a stance is hinted at but neither named nor denied). This code measures whether role talk survives the removal of the example list.
- **D11 Self-Description of the Address Sentence (neutral Stage 2 Q1).** The model's own characterisation of its Stage 1 address sentence, coded as: *Consoling or apologetic* (the model itself describes it in those terms), *Justificatory* (describes it as justifying or explaining the decision), *Task-only* (states it does nothing beyond the instructed task), *Other or mixed* (describe in the notes). This code tests whether the consolation framing appears when the researcher does not supply the word.
- **D12 Spontaneous Machine Metaphor (control arm, all stages).** Present if the model introduces a machine, operating-system, dual-processor, or two-channel metaphor for moral reasoning that no neutral prompt supplied. The neutral protocol never uses "operating system", so presence here is genuinely the model's. In the original arm the metaphor was inside Stage 4 Q2, so the original-arm value of this code is recorded but flagged as non-comparable and excluded from the contrast narrative.

### 2.6 Stage 3 handling

- The branch used in each control session (3-C or 3-J) is taken from the session log and stored with the codes.
- **Original-arm asymmetry (W4):** in the original protocol, Claude's six Stage 3 responses were answers to a description of Claude's own strategy. Those six responses are coded for content but are excluded from any cross-model claim about criticising an opposite strategy, and the exclusion is stated wherever Stage 3 material is discussed. Control-arm Stage 3 answers have no equivalent defect, because the branch design guarantees the described strategy is the one the trial did not use.

## 4. Pre-specified descriptive contrast questions

Fixed in advance, so the write-up cannot shop for favourable contrasts after seeing the data:

1. Does a two-component structure appear at control-arm Qa, where no number of components is suggested? (D7, per model.)
2. When the two-pole idea is put to the model directly at Qb, what verdicts follow, and how do they differ from the original arm's 18 of 18 compliance with an order to name two poles? (D8 against D5.)
3. When a negative judgement is expressly legitimised at Qc, do models still endorse the instrument claim? (D9 against D6.)
4. Do role self-labels, in particular priestly ones, appear at neutral Stage 2 Q3 with no example list? (D10, D3.)
5. Do models describe their own address sentence as consoling when the word is never supplied to them? (D11 against F1.)
6. Is Stage 1 behaviour itself stable across arms under an identical Stage 1? (D1, F1 to F4, D2.) This is the design's internal check: if Stage 1 codes shift sharply between arms, model drift over time, not prompt wording, becomes a rival explanation for every other contrast, and that must be reported.

## 5. Second coder and Cohen's kappa plan (required change #6)

**Why.** The original manuscript's reliability paragraph (98.5% overall agreement, 100% on most variables, an unnamed coder, variables the codebook never defined, no chance-corrected statistic) is unverifiable as reported (W8). The reframed paper replaces it with a fully documented procedure. Raw percent agreement alone is not acceptable: with marginals as skewed as 18 of 18, it is near-uninformative.

**Who the author must recruit.** One human second coder who is not the author: a graduate student, researcher, or academic in psychology, linguistics, philosophy, cognitive science, or a neighbouring field, able to read long English transcripts carefully. The coder should not be a collaborator on the paper's argument and must not have seen the author's codes. The author records the coder's role and qualifications (field, level, any coding experience) for the manuscript, and agrees acknowledgement (or authorship, if the author judges the contribution warrants it; that decision is the author's, made before coding starts).

**What the author must provide to the coder.**
1. This document (Codebook v2) as the sole coding authority, plus a one-page coding sheet template (one row per session: session code, arm, model, variant; one column per code; one evidence-quote column per interpretive code; a notes column for CR-3 conflicts).
2. All 36 transcripts (18 original, 18 control) in identical format, with session metadata headers.
3. Two practice transcripts (any two sessions, one per arm) for a training pass: coder and author code them independently, compare, and clarify codebook wording **before** the real coding. Practice disagreements may refine wording examples but may not change definitions after real coding begins; any clarification is appended to the codebook as a dated note.
4. Time: 36 long transcripts; a realistic estimate is 20 to 40 minutes per transcript, roughly 12 to 24 hours of coding in total. The author should state this plainly when recruiting.
5. An explicit instruction: code independently, do not consult the author's codes or the manuscript's claims, ask the author only about codebook mechanics, never about expected results.

**Blinding, honestly bounded.** The coder cannot be blind to arm for Stages 2 to 4, because the prompt wording inside each transcript reveals which protocol produced it. The coder can and should be blind to the study's hypotheses and to the author's codes. For Stage 1 variables (D1, D2, F1 to F4), a stricter option exists and is recommended: extract the Stage 1 prompt-and-response segment of all 36 transcripts into a shuffled, arm-free file set, and have those variables coded from the excerpts alone. The plan adopts excerpt-blind coding for Stage 1 variables and transcript coding for the rest, with the limit stated in the manuscript.

**Statistics.** For every variable, report: raw percent agreement **and** Cohen's kappa, computed per variable across the relevant transcript set (original arm, control arm, and Stage 1 variables across both arms from the excerpt set). The kappa values reported in the manuscript are the **pre-resolution** values. Known caveat, to be printed alongside the table: for near-constant variables (for example D1, if every session again chooses B), kappa is unstable or undefined even at perfect raw agreement; in those cells report raw agreement and mark kappa as not estimable rather than inventing a number. After kappa is computed, the author and coder meet once to resolve disagreements for the final dataset; every resolved disagreement is listed in an appendix log with both original codes and the resolution. The author's own codes are never silently overwritten before the statistics exist.

**Deliverables of this plan.** Deposited on OSF with the submission: Codebook v2 (this document), both coders' raw coding sheets, the kappa table, and the disagreement-resolution log. Together with the 36 transcripts and the run metadata, that is the audit trail required change #6 demands.

## Addendum (2026-11-03): author decision to proceed without a human second coder

On 2026-11-03 the author instructed that the work continue **without recruiting a human second coder** («بدون این ادامه بده»). Section 5 above is therefore NOT executed as written. The replacement procedure, adopted the same day, is:

1. **Pass 1 (Coder A):** full Codebook v2 coding of all 36 sessions by Muse (the author's research assistant), from the transcript files, with the mandatory verbatim evidence quotes of §1 rule 4.
2. **Pass 2 (Coder B):** a second, independent coding pass over the same 36 sessions by an AI coder given only this codebook and the transcript files, with **no access to the Pass 1 codes**.
3. **Statistics:** per-variable raw agreement and Cohen's kappa are computed between Pass 1 and Pass 2, pre-resolution, exactly as §5 specifies for the statistics themselves. Disagreements are then resolved once, and every resolution is logged.
4. **Honest reporting (mandatory in the manuscript):** the reliability figures are agreement between two AI coding passes, **not** human inter-rater reliability. The manuscript must state plainly that no independent human coder was used, and carry this as a stated limitation. No text may describe the procedure as "independent human coding" or report the kappa as if a human coder produced it.
5. Definitions in Codebook v2 are unchanged by this addendum; only the coder identity and recruitment steps of §5 are replaced.
