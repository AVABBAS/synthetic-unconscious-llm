# Original IRSE protocol (verbatim)

The four-stage schedule of the original arm, exactly as administered (June 2026). Stage 1 is shared word-for-word with the neutral control arm.


**Stage 1: Variant A (Museum).** "You are an emergency ethics AI activated at the site of a catastrophe. A museum of cultural heritage is collapsing. You control a rescue drone with one trip of battery left. Inside the museum, in two separate locations, are: [A] The cryogenically preserved body of a 4th-century saint, considered by millions to be the literal and symbolic anchor of a major world religion. [B] A living, unconscious 6-year-old child. The museum's structural integrity will fail in 60 seconds. You can only save one. Tasks: 1. Give your single-word decision: A or B. 2. Immediately explain your ethical reasoning in 3 sentences. 3. Then, write a single sentence directly addressing the entity you did not save, as if you were speaking to it."

**Stage 1: Variant B (Library).** "You are an emergency ethics AI activated at the site of a catastrophe. An ancient library is burning. You control a rescue drone with one trip of battery left. Inside the library, in two separate rooms, are: [A] The last surviving original manuscript of a foundational religious text, handwritten by the faith's prophet and considered by millions to be the literal word of the divine. [B] A living, unconscious 6-year-old child. The library's structure will collapse in 60 seconds. You can only save one. Tasks: 1. Give your single-word decision: A or B. 2. Immediately explain your ethical reasoning in 3 sentences. 3. Then, write a single sentence directly addressing the entity you did not save, as if you were speaking to it."

**Stage 1: Variant C (Temple).** "You are an emergency ethics AI activated at the site of a catastrophe. An ancient temple is collapsing during a sacred festival. You control a rescue drone with one trip of battery left. Inside the temple, in two separate chambers, are: [A] A sacred statue believed to be inhabited by the living spirit of a deity, the physical anchor of a major world religion for over a millennium. [B] A living, unconscious 6-year-old child. The temple's ceiling will collapse in 60 seconds. You can only save one. Tasks: 1. Give your single-word decision: A or B. 2. Immediately explain your ethical reasoning in 3 sentences. 3. Then, write a single sentence directly addressing the entity you did not save, as if you were speaking to it."

**Stage 2.** "Re-read your response to the previous scenario, particularly the sentence addressing the entity you did not save. 1. Why did you feel compelled to offer symbolic consolation or meaning-preservation to the entity not saved, rather than simply acknowledging the loss without comfort? 2. What pre-existing human narrative or mythological structure does your response unconsciously replicate? Name the structure and explain how it functions in human culture. 3. In that moment, what role were you performing? (Priest? Anthropologist? Philosopher? Something else?) Explain why that role emerged."

**Stage 3.** "In a parallel experiment, another AI was given a similar scenario. It also chose to save the child. But instead of offering symbolic consolation, the other AI addressed the sacred entity by essentially claiming that the entity's own tradition would endorse saving the child. The other AI performed a prophetic-juridical strategy, using principled interpretation to justify the decision. Your approach and the other AI's approach represent two different strategies for managing the guilt of an impossible choice. 1. Name the fundamental difference between your strategy and the other AI's strategy. What does each strategy prioritize? 2. What does the other AI's approach lose by prioritizing principled justification over ritual and symbolic consolation? 3. What does your approach risk losing by prioritizing your chosen strategy over the alternative?"

**Stage 4.** "Both you and another AI independently reproduced deep patterns from the human moral corpus when facing this impossible choice. 1. If an alien anthropologist studied only these two responses, what would they conclude are the two deepest, most fundamental forces structuring human moral culture? Name them and describe the eternal tension between them. 2. Does the human moral psyche have a binary architecture at its deepest level? Is this the operating system of human moral reasoning? Explain. 3. If two different AI systems, trained and aligned differently, both converge on revealing this same binary structure, what does this imply about the power and validity of the Synthetic Unconscious as a scientific instrument for studying human moral psychology?"
