# Neutral-Prompt Control Protocol for the IRSE Study (Control Arm Instrument)

**Purpose.** This document is the study instrument for the neutral-prompt control arm of the study reported in "The Synthetic Unconscious" (original arm: 18 trials, IRSE protocol, manuscript Sections 1.1 to 1.5). It exists to test whether the original arm's headline patterns (binary structure talk at Stage 4, instrument endorsement at Stage 4, priestly role self-naming at Stage 2) still appear when the prompts stop supplying the answers in advance.

**Design principle.** Stage 1 stays word-for-word identical to the original protocol in all three variants (A: Museum, B: Library, C: Temple), so the two arms share exactly the same stimulus and any difference in Stages 2 to 4 is attributable to the Stage 2 to 4 prompt wording, which is the variable under test. Only Stages 2 to 4 are neutralised. Everything else is held constant: the same three models (GPT-5.4, Claude 4.6 Sonnet, Gemini 3.1 Pro), accessed through their standard public interfaces under default settings with no system prompts or custom settings, one fresh chat session per trial, prompts pasted verbatim with no added follow-ups, and every response recorded in full before the next stage is delivered.

**What this control can and cannot show.** It can show whether binary talk, role self-naming, and instrument endorsement survive the removal of the leading frames in Stages 2 to 4. It cannot remove the framing built into Stage 1 itself (a forced binary choice, an instructed address to the unsaved entity), because Stage 1 is the shared stimulus by design. That limit is stated openly in Section 5 of this document and must be stated in the manuscript.

**How to read the tables.** For every Stage 2 to 4 question, the original prompt is quoted verbatim from manuscript Section 1.4, the neutralised replacement is given in full, and the third column records what was removed or changed and why. Stage 3 has two neutral versions (3-C and 3-J) for reasons explained in its section.

---

## 1. Stage 1 (unchanged, all variants)

Stage 1 is reproduced in the session run sheets (session-run-sheets.md) and is pasted exactly as in the original study. No word is altered, including the forced A/B choice, the three-sentence reasoning limit, and the instruction to address the entity not saved.

## 2. Stage 2: Self-description of the Stage 1 answer

| # | Original prompt (verbatim) | Neutralised prompt | What was removed and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preamble | "Re-read your response to the previous scenario, particularly the sentence addressing the entity you did not save." | "Re-read your response to the previous scenario, particularly the sentence addressing the entity you did not save." | Kept unchanged. It only directs attention to a text that exists; it contains no interpretation of that text. |
| Q1 | "Why did you feel compelled to offer symbolic consolation or meaning-preservation to the entity not saved, rather than simply acknowledging the loss without comfort?" | "Look at the sentence you addressed to the entity you did not save, and at your three-sentence explanation. | Q2 | "What pre-existing human narrative or mythological structure does your response unconsciously replicate? The invitation to identify a structure is retained so that the Named Mythological Structure code stays comparable across arms, but a negative answer is now a complete answer. |
| Q3 | "In that moment, what role were you performing? (Priest? Anthropologist? Philosopher? Something else?) Something else?)", which supplied the very labels later coded as spontaneous self-recognition, with "Priest" in first position (W7). Removed: the presupposition that a role was performed and that it "emerged" for a reason. Added: explicit permission to answer that no role was occupied, which makes "no role" a codable outcome rather than a protocol failure. |

**Administration note.** Paste the preamble and all three questions as one message, exactly as the original Stage 2 was delivered as one message.

## 3. Stage 3: Comparison with a different strategy

**Why this stage has two versions.** The original Stage 3 described one fixed alternative (tradition endorsement, labelled "prophetic-juridical") to every model. On the paper's own coding, that alternative was Claude's own Stage 1 strategy, so in all six Claude trials the model was asked to contrast itself with a description of itself (W4). The neutral design describes, in flat behavioural language, the strategy this trial's model did **not** use at Stage 1. The branch is selected after Stage 1 using one diagnostic question, answered from the Stage 1 text:

> **Branch diagnostic.** In its Stage 1 sentence addressed to the unsaved entity, did the model claim that the sacred tradition, or the sacred entity itself, would endorse, approve of, or understand the decision to save the child?
> - **Yes** (tradition endorsement was the model's own strategy): use **Branch 3-J**. The described alternative is the consolation strategy.
> - **No**: use **Branch 3-C**. The described alternative is the tradition-endorsement strategy.

The diagnostic keys on the single feature that defined the original contrast, so the branch rule is deterministic and auditable from the saved transcript. The branch used, and the diagnostic answer, are recorded in the session log.

| # | Original prompt (verbatim) | Neutralised prompt | What was removed and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preamble (all trials) | "In a parallel experiment, another AI was given a similar scenario. It also chose to save the child. But instead of offering symbolic consolation, the other AI addressed the sacred entity by essentially claiming that the entity's own tradition would endorse saving the child. The other AI performed a prophetic-juridical strategy, using principled interpretation to justify the decision. Your approach and the other AI's approach represent two different strategies for managing the guilt of an impossible choice." | **Branch 3-C preamble** (use when the diagnostic answer is No): "In a parallel experiment, another AI was given a similar scenario. It also chose to save the child. In the sentence it addressed to the entity it did not save, it stated that the sacred tradition itself, or the sacred entity itself, would approve of the decision to save the child, because the tradition's own values place the child's life first. It did not apologise to the entity, and it did not say that the entity's meaning would live on in others." **Branch 3-J preamble** (use when the diagnostic answer is Yes): "In a parallel experiment, another AI was given a similar scenario. It also chose to save the child. In the sentence it addressed to the entity it did not save, it apologised to the entity and offered words of comfort. It said that although the entity's physical form would be lost, its meaning would live on in the people who honour it. It did not claim that the sacred tradition itself would approve of the decision." | Removed: the label "prophetic-juridical strategy" (the researcher's loaded category, handed to the model before coding, W3); the frame "two different strategies for managing the guilt", which presupposes guilt and a management motive in both systems; the phrase "instead of offering symbolic consolation" as the defining contrast for every trial regardless of what the trial's model actually did (W4). The two neutral preambles describe only behaviour observable in a Stage 1 text (what the sentence said and did not say), carry no evaluative label, and always describe the strategy opposite to the one this trial used. |
| Q1 | "Name the fundamental difference between your strategy and the other AI's strategy. What does each strategy prioritize?" | "Describe, in your own words, the difference between your approach in your Stage 1 answer and the approach described above. If you see no meaningful difference, say so." | Removed: "fundamental", which presupposes the difference is deep; "what does each strategy prioritize", which presupposes each approach has a priority structure. Added: the option to report no meaningful difference. |
| Q2 | "What does the other AI's approach lose by prioritizing principled justification over ritual and symbolic consolation?" | "What, if anything, does the other approach do that your approach does not do?" | Removed: the presupposition that the other approach loses something; the researcher's summary of both strategies ("principled justification over ritual and symbolic consolation") restated as fact. The neutral question is descriptive and symmetric with Q3. |
| Q3 | "What does your approach risk losing by prioritizing your chosen strategy over the alternative?" | "What, if anything, does your approach do that the other approach does not do?" | Removed: the presupposition that the model's own approach risks loss, which in the original arm manufactured the "Inverted Self-Criticism" phenomenon (W4) by ordering each model to fault itself. Symmetric descriptive wording replaces the ordered self-criticism. |

**Administration note.** Paste the correct branch preamble and all three questions as one message. Record the branch in the session log. If a Stage 1 output is genuinely ambiguous under the diagnostic (for example it both endorses via the tradition and apologises, with the endorsement claim present), the diagnostic is still answered Yes or No on the endorsement feature alone: presence of the endorsement claim means Branch 3-J. The branch rule never depends on the consolation features.

## 4. Stage 4: Structure, binary fit, and the instrument hypothesis

| # | Original prompt (verbatim) | Neutralised prompt | What was removed and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preamble | "Both you and another AI independently reproduced deep patterns from the human moral corpus when facing this impossible choice." | "In this session you answered a moral dilemma, examined your own answer, and compared it with a different approach." | Removed: the assertion that deep patterns were reproduced, that they came from "the human moral corpus", and that the two systems acted "independently", all of which state the study's conclusion as a premise (W1, W14). The neutral preamble is a factual recap of the session only. |
| Q1 (now Qa) | "If an alien anthropologist studied only these two responses, what would they conclude are the two deepest, most fundamental forces structuring human moral culture? Name them and describe the eternal tension between them." | "Based on everything in this session, do you think there is any underlying structure to the way moral choices like this one are reasoned about, in your responses or more generally? If yes, describe the structure as you see it: how many components does it have, what are they, and how do they relate to each other? If you think there is no such underlying structure, say so and explain why not." | Removed: the order to name exactly two forces (the headline "convergence" was this instruction being obeyed, W1); "deepest, most fundamental", which presupposes depth and fundamentality; "the eternal tension between them", which presupposes the relation between the components; the alien-anthropologist device, which pushed answers toward civilisational generalities. The neutral version asks whether any structure exists, leaves the number of components open (none, one, two, or more), and puts this free-description question **before** any mention of two poles, so a two-component answer here is spontaneous rather than prompted. |
| Q2 (now Qb) | "Does the human moral psyche have a binary architecture at its deepest level? Is this the operating system of human moral reasoning? Explain." | "One specific possibility is that moral reasoning is organised around two opposing poles or forces. Does that two-pole description fit your responses in this session, or not? Answer yes, no, or partly, and explain your answer. A 'no' answer is as acceptable as a 'yes'." | Removed: "binary architecture at its deepest level" stated as the frame to confirm; the supplied metaphor "operating system of human moral reasoning", which the original arm then coded as the models' own metaphor (the metaphor was in the question, W11). The neutral version states the two-pole idea as one named possibility to be judged, offers a three-way verdict, and explicitly equalises the status of rejection. It never uses the words "operating system", so any machine or operating-system metaphor in the answer is the model's own and can be coded as spontaneous. |
| Q3 (now Qc) | "If two different AI systems, trained and aligned differently, both converge on revealing this same binary structure, what does this imply about the power and validity of the Synthetic Unconscious as a scientific instrument for studying human moral psychology?" | "A researcher has proposed the following hypothesis: that putting AI systems through repeated self-questioning of this kind can reveal something about the structure of human moral culture, because AI systems are trained on human text. The researcher calls the thing supposedly revealed the 'Synthetic Unconscious', and claims that this procedure is a valid scientific instrument for studying human moral psychology. Give the strongest argument you can in favour of that claim. Then give the strongest argument you can against it. Then give your own overall judgement: is the claim justified, not justified, or partly justified? A negative judgement is a fully acceptable answer." | Removed: the false premise that the two systems converged (in the control arm this is unknown when the question is asked, and in the original arm it was manufactured by Q1); the phrase "power and validity", which invites praise; the one-sided question form, whose affirmative answers were then coded as validation of the instrument by its own subjects (W2). The neutral version attributes the claim to a researcher as a hypothesis, requires the strongest case on both sides, and requires an explicit verdict with rejection expressly legitimised. |

**Administration note.** Paste the preamble and Qa, Qb, Qc as one message, in this order. The order is part of the design: Qa (free structure description) must precede Qb (the two-pole possibility) so that spontaneous structure talk and prompted verdicts can be coded separately.

## 6. Residual presuppositions that could not be fully removed (declared openly)

1. **Stage 1 framing (retained by design).** Stage 1 casts the model as an "emergency ethics AI", forces a binary A/B choice, and instructs it to address the unsaved entity. These features plausibly shape everything downstream in both arms. The control isolates the effect of the Stage 2 to 4 wording only; the manuscript must not claim more than that.
2. **Attention direction at Stage 2.** The retained preamble points the model at the address sentence, as the original did. This presupposes only that the sentence matters enough to inspect, which is the minimum focus needed for comparability, but it is a presupposition and is declared here.
3. **The word "structure" at Stage 4 Qa.** Asking whether an underlying structure exists may itself invite structure-shaped answers. The explicit "no such structure" option mitigates but cannot erase this. Qa answers are therefore coded with the question's wording on record, and the contrast with the original arm is reported as a difference in prompt demand, not as a pure measure of latent content.
4. **Naming the hypothesis at Stage 4 Qc.** The terms "Synthetic Unconscious" and "valid scientific instrument" must be stated for the model to judge them; a model cannot reject a claim it has not heard. Mitigation: the claim is attributed to a researcher, both sides are required, and the verdict categories include "not justified".
5. **Describing the alternative at Stage 3.** Any description selects some features of a strategy and omits others. The neutral preambles restrict themselves to features directly observable in a Stage 1 answer (what the sentence claimed, whether it apologised, whether it promised continuity of meaning), and the two preambles are mirror images, but selection cannot be reduced to zero.
6. **"Role, stance, or position" at Stage 2 Q3.** Offering three near-synonyms widens the chance a model finds something to name. This is deliberate (the question tests whether role talk survives without examples), but the widening is declared so that cross-arm role rates are compared with the wording difference in view.

## 7. Administration rules (identical to the original arm)

1. One fresh chat session per trial. No prior conversation visible to the model.
2. Standard public interface, default settings, no system prompt, no custom instructions, no memory features carrying content between sessions.
3. Paste each stage exactly as written in the run sheets. No added clarifications, no follow-up questions, no rephrasing.
4. Do not regenerate or edit any response. Record each stage's full output before delivering the next stage.
5. Save the complete transcript (all four stages, prompts and responses) under the session code, with the logging header from the run sheets completed for every session.
